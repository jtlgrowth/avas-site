Put them to work.

You hired an employee this afternoon. It has a name and a seat. Right now it is sitting there with nothing to do. Here is how you give it a job, and the one rule that makes it yours.

The activity · 20 minutes

Give it a job and a rule.

Not a task. A job has a name, a scope and a standard. A rule is the one thing it must never do, in your words. Write both, run it once, then fix the rule and run it again.

20:00 Twenty minutes. The clock is the point.
1

Open your terminal

Laptop, not your phone.

0–2 min
Windows

Press the Windows key, type PowerShell, press Enter.

Mac

Press Cmd and Space, type Terminal, press Enter.

Then type these three lines, one at a time, pressing Enter after each.

terminal
cd Desktop
mkdir my-employee
cd my-employee

That makes one folder for this employee to live in. A folder is a department.

4

Give it real work

Not a test. Something from today.

8–13 min

Paste this next, with your own real thing in the brackets. A real client thread, a real enquiry, a real half-written post.

paste into claude

[paste the actual thing from your business]

When you are done, tell me which part of my rule

It will be roughly right and specifically wrong. That is normal, and it is the whole point of the next step.

5

Fix the rule, not the output

This is the step everybody skips.

13–17 min

Do not rewrite the draft yourself. Quote the line you hated and make it change the rule instead.

paste into claude
This line was wrong: "[paste the exact line you hated]"

Here is how I would have written it: "[write it your way]"

Do not just fix the draft. Tell me which part of my rule
let that through, rewrite the rule, then run the job again.

Fix the output and you fix it again tomorrow. Fix the rule and it is gone forever.

6

Make it permanent

So you never explain any of this again.

17–20 min
paste into claude
Save everything we agreed into a file called CLAUDE.md
in this folder, so every future session already knows it.

Include the job, the final rule, how I write, and the
things I never want to see. Keep it under 400 words.
Then show me the file.

Done when you have one output on screen you would actually send today, and a CLAUDE.md file sitting in that folder.

Steal one of these

A job and a rule, in eight businesses.

Find the one closest to yours, then change the nouns. The rule is what makes it yours, so do not copy that part.

Agency · OBM
Job
You write the weekly client update. Five bullets, what shipped, what is next, what I need from them.
Rule
Never write "we are excited to". If something slipped, say it slipped in the first bullet.
Travel
Job
You draft the itinerary from a client brief. Day by day, with my supplier rates already in it.
Rule
Never invent a price. If you do not have the rate, write RATE NEEDED in caps.
Skincare · Ecommerce
Job
You answer the five questions that come into my DMs every day, in my voice, ready to send.
Rule
Never promise a result or a timeline. Never diagnose. Point to the consult if they push.
Coaching
Job
You turn my call notes into the client recap email. What we decided, what they owe me, by when.
Rule
Never use the word journey. Never soften a deadline into a suggestion.
Clinic
Job
You write the pre-appointment message. What to bring, what to expect, how long it takes.
Rule
Never give medical advice. Never use a patient's name in anything you draft for social.
Real estate
Job
You rank my new leads and tell me who to call first, with one line on why each one ranks there.
Rule
Never rank on budget alone. Somebody ready this month beats somebody rich next year.
Food · Retail
Job
You cost a new menu item from my supplier list and give me three price points with the margin on each.
Rule
Never round down. Always show the labour line even when it is small.
Any business
Job
You write my Monday post from what actually happened last week.
Rule
Never open with a question. Never use an em dash. Short lines, the way I text.
01

Every problem is one of five jobs.

You do not have a hundred problems. You have one of five, repeated. Pick one today, not five.

The SorterDecides what is worth your time and throws out the rest.
The FinderGoes looking so you stop scrolling. Leads, suppliers, rates, PR spots.
The WriterProduces the thing you rewrite every week. Captions, proposals, replies.
The AnswererHandles the same questions you answer forty times a month.
The WatcherTells you when something changes. Stock, reviews, deadlines, follow-ups gone cold.

03

How to talk to it.

  • Give the context once, not every time.Your prices, your clients, your voice, the mistake you made in July. Paste it once and stop re-explaining every morning.
  • Show it what done looks like.One real example of your own work that worked beats every adjective. "Warm and professional" means nothing. Your best DM means everything.
  • Correct the rule, not the output.Fix the draft yourself and you will fix it again tomorrow. Fix the rule and it is gone forever.
  • Say what you do not want.Everybody skips this one. Two or three things you hate seeing will save you more time than a page of instructions.
  • Ask it questions, not just orders."Which of these should I do first, and why" is when it stops being a chatbot and starts being the person you think out loud with.
  • Same seat, same thread.A new chat every time throws the training away. One seat, one thread, and it compounds.
04

When you get stuck.

  • It sounds like a robot.You never gave it a Sounds like. Paste one real message of yours and ask it to write ten rules about how you write.
  • It keeps making things up.Your rule has no limit in it. Add: if you do not have the number, write NEEDED in caps instead of guessing.
  • It says it cannot.Half the time it can. Ask what exactly is stopping it, whether that is a real limit, and to do the part it can while telling you what is left.
  • It forgot everything.You opened a new chat. Go back to the same thread, or run prompt 05 so the rules are saved permanently.
  • You are still stuck.Ask in the Robots & Coffee community. Somebody in there has already hit it.
