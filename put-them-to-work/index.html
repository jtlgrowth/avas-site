Put them to work.

You hired an employee this morning. It has a name, a seat and nothing to do. This afternoon is the other half of the job: how to actually hand work over, so it comes back the way you would have done it.

The activity · 20 minutes

Hand one real job over.

You already hired your AI employee this morning. Nobody is hiring again. This is the part nobody teaches: how to actually hand work to it and give it the rules that make it sound like you. One real job, properly delegated, in twenty minutes.

20:00 Twenty minutes. The clock is the point.
1

Open your AI employee

The one you hired this morning. Laptop, not your phone.

0–2 min

First open the black window you used this morning. It is called the terminal. It is just a place to type.

Windows

Press the Windows key, type PowerShell, press Enter.

Mac

Press Cmd and Space, type Terminal, press Enter.

A window opens with a cursor blinking. Type this one word and press Enter.

terminal
claude
What you should seeSome text, then a box at the bottom waiting for you to type. That box is your employee. Everything from here gets typed or pasted into it.

Now ask it who it is, so you know you are talking to the right one. Paste this in and press Enter. Paste is Ctrl+V on Windows, Cmd+V on Mac.

paste into claude
Who did I hire you as this morning?
Tell me your job in one sentence.

It says it does not know? Then it is a fresh one. That is fine and it takes ten seconds to fix. Type: “You are the [name] I set up this morning. Your job is [job].” Press Enter and carry on. That is all hiring ever was.

It says command not found? Raise your hand. Do not sit quietly and lose the twenty minutes.

2

Pick what you are handing over

One job. Not your whole business.

2–4 min

Think about last week, not next week. Not the hard things. The things you did again that you had also done the week before. Replying to the same enquiry. Writing the same kind of message. Chasing the same kind of person.

Almost everybody picks something too big the first time. Here is the difference:

Too big“Handle my marketing.” “Grow my sales.” “Manage my clients.” Nobody could do these from one instruction, including a human.
The right size“The follow-up message when someone goes quiet.” “The reply to a price enquiry.” “The caption for a before and after.” One message, one output.

Now run the one you picked through these three questions. In your head, quickly.

Does it repeat?If no, stop. Never delegate something you only do once.
Can I show an example of “done”?If no, you cannot brief it yet. Pick something else.
Am I the only one who can judge it?Then keep the decision, and hand over the draft.

It repeats, and you can show an example of done. That is the one you hand over today.

3

Write the five-line brief

This is the whole skill. Everything else is typing.

4–11 min

You are about to do the thing most people never do. Instead of asking it for something, you are going to brief it, the way you would brief a new team member on their first day.

  1. Fill in the boxes below. Short answers. Do not write an essay.
  2. The grey box underneath fills itself in as you type. That is your brief.
  3. Press Copy my brief, then paste it into your terminal and press Enter.

One task, one output. Say what it produces, not what it is responsible for. “Writes the follow-up message”, not “handles follow-ups”.

How long, what shape, where it goes. If you cannot say when it is finished, it will never be finished. This is the line that stops it writing you a paragraph when you wanted two lines.

The facts it cannot know. Paste the actual thread, the actual price, the actual dates. It has never met your client. Everything you leave out, it will invent.

Go into your own messages, find one you actually sent that worked, and paste it. Do not describe your voice. Saying “friendly but professional” tells it nothing. One real message tells it everything.

The two or three things that make you cringe. Be petty. Petty is specific, and specific is what it can follow.

Fill in the job and done-when. Your brief appears here, ready to paste.
Copied

Stuck on a box? Leave it empty and keep going. Job and done-when are the only two that have to be there.

4

Hand it off and read what comes back

Send it. Then stop typing and actually read.

11–14 min

Paste the brief into the terminal and press Enter. Give it ten or twenty seconds. You will see it thinking, then the answer appears.

What you should seeSomething roughly right and specifically wrong. Close enough that you are a bit impressed, off enough that you would not send it as it is. That is exactly what is supposed to happen.

Now do the part that matters. Read it properly and find one line you would never have sent. Not the whole thing. One line. Put your finger on it.

It came back perfect? Then your brief was good. Give it a harder one, or a real thread you have been avoiding all week.

It came back nothing like you wanted? Good, that is the more useful outcome, and step 5 is where it gets fixed.

5

Give it the rule

Correct the brief, not the draft. This is the step everybody skips.

14–18 min

Here is where nearly everyone goes wrong. They see a line they do not like, quietly fix it themselves, send it, and feel like AI saved them five minutes. Next week they fix the same line again.

Do not touch the draft. Quote the line you hated and turn it into a rule it keeps forever.

paste into claude
This line was wrong: "[paste the exact line you hated]"

Here is how I would have written it: "[write it your way]"

Do not just fix the draft. Tell me which line of my brief
let that through, rewrite that line, then run the job again.
What you should seeIt tells you which part of your brief was too loose, rewrites that line, and runs the job again. The second version should sound noticeably more like you. That gap is the entire lesson of this afternoon.

Fix the draft and you fix it again next week. Fix the rule and it is gone for good. That is the difference between using AI and managing it.

6

Make it permanent

So you never brief this job twice.

18–20 min

Everything you just agreed only lives in this conversation. Close the window and it is gone. This last step writes it into a note your employee reads every single time it wakes up.

paste into claude
Save this job into a file called CLAUDE.md, so you
never have to brief me on it again.

Include the final five-line brief, how I write, and the
things I never want to see. Keep it under 400 words.
Then show me the file.
What you should seeIt writes the file and prints it back to you. Read it. If something in there is not true about you, tell it now and make it fix the file.

Done when you have one output on screen you would actually send today, and your employee has the rule saved so it never makes that mistake again.

Steal one of these

A job and a rule, in eight businesses.

Find the one closest to yours, then change the nouns. The rule is what makes it yours, so do not copy that part.

Agency · OBM
Job
You write the weekly client update. Five bullets, what shipped, what is next, what I need from them.
Rule
Never write "we are excited to". If something slipped, say it slipped in the first bullet.
Travel
Job
You draft the itinerary from a client brief. Day by day, with my supplier rates already in it.
Rule
Never invent a price. If you do not have the rate, write RATE NEEDED in caps.
Skincare · Ecommerce
Job
You answer the five questions that come into my DMs every day, in my voice, ready to send.
Rule
Never promise a result or a timeline. Never diagnose. Point to the consult if they push.
Coaching
Job
You turn my call notes into the client recap email. What we decided, what they owe me, by when.
Rule
Never use the word journey. Never soften a deadline into a suggestion.
Clinic
Job
You write the pre-appointment message. What to bring, what to expect, how long it takes.
Rule
Never give medical advice. Never use a patient's name in anything you draft for social.
Real estate
Job
You rank my new leads and tell me who to call first, with one line on why each one ranks there.
Rule
Never rank on budget alone. Somebody ready this month beats somebody rich next year.
Food · Retail
Job
You cost a new menu item from my supplier list and give me three price points with the margin on each.
Rule
Never round down. Always show the labour line even when it is small.
Any business
Job
You write my Monday post from what actually happened last week.
Rule
Never open with a question. Never use an em dash. Short lines, the way I text.
01

Every problem is one of five jobs.

You do not have a hundred problems. You have one of five, repeated. Pick one today, not five.

The SorterDecides what is worth your time and throws out the rest.
The FinderGoes looking so you stop scrolling. Leads, suppliers, rates, PR spots.
The WriterProduces the thing you rewrite every week. Captions, proposals, replies.
The AnswererHandles the same questions you answer forty times a month.
The WatcherTells you when something changes. Stock, reviews, deadlines, follow-ups gone cold.

03

How to talk to it.

  • Give the context once, not every time.Your prices, your clients, your voice, the mistake you made in July. Paste it once and stop re-explaining every morning.
  • Show it what done looks like.One real example of your own work that worked beats every adjective. "Warm and professional" means nothing. Your best DM means everything.
  • Correct the rule, not the output.Fix the draft yourself and you will fix it again tomorrow. Fix the rule and it is gone forever.
  • Say what you do not want.Everybody skips this one. Two or three things you hate seeing will save you more time than a page of instructions.
  • Ask it questions, not just orders."Which of these should I do first, and why" is when it stops being a chatbot and starts being the person you think out loud with.
  • Same seat, same thread.A new chat every time throws the training away. One seat, one thread, and it compounds.
04

When you get stuck.

  • It sounds like a robot.You never gave it a Sounds like. Paste one real message of yours and ask it to write ten rules about how you write.
  • It keeps making things up.Your rule has no limit in it. Add: if you do not have the number, write NEEDED in caps instead of guessing.
  • It says it cannot.Half the time it can. Ask what exactly is stopping it, whether that is a real limit, and to do the part it can while telling you what is left.
  • It forgot everything.You opened a new chat. Go back to the same thread, or run prompt 05 so the rules are saved permanently.
  • You are still stuck.Ask in the Robots & Coffee community. Somebody in there has already hit it.
