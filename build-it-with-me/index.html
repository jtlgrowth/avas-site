Make the place the emails live

Before a workflow can send an email, the email has to exist as a template.

10 min
  1. Open your Marketing area, then Emails, then Templates. This is the folder where your saved emails live.
  2. Create a new template. Name it exactly 01 Welcome. Numbering them keeps them in order forever. Do this and thank yourself in six months.
  3. Paste Email 1 from your emails.md file into it. Paste the subject line too. Save.

2

Build the workflow

A workflow is just: when this happens, do these things, in this order.

20 min
  1. Go to Automation, then Workflows, then create a new one and start from scratch. Name it after the offer, not after the tool. "Free blueprint signups", not "Workflow 1".
  2. Add the Trigger. Choose Form Submitted and pick the form on your landing page. The trigger is the thing that wakes the machine up. Nothing runs until this happens.
  3. Add an Action. Choose Add Tag. Type a tag like lead-blueprint. A tag is a sticker on a person. Later you can find everyone with that sticker in one click.
  4. Add an Action. Choose Send Email. Pick 01 Welcome. This one has no wait before it. It goes out the second they sign up, while they still remember you.
  5. Add a Wait. Set it to 2 days. Wait means exactly that. The machine pauses, then carries on by itself.
  6. Add Send Email → 02 Objection. Then Wait 3 days.
  7. Add Send Email → 03 Proof. Then Wait 3 days.
  8. Add Send Email → 04 Offer. Then Wait 3 days.
  9. Add Send Email → 05 Close. That is the whole sequence. Nine steps and it runs for eleven days without you.
  10. Turn the workflow from Draft to Publish. If you forget this, nothing sends. This is the single most common mistake, including by people who have done it before.
Test it before you trust itFill in your own form with your own email address. Email 1 should land in your inbox within a minute or two. If it does not, the workflow is still in Draft, or the trigger is pointing at a different form.
3

Stop emailing people who already bought

The most embarrassing mistake in automation, and the easiest to prevent.

5 min
  1. In the same workflow, open the Settings and find Remove from workflow conditions.
  2. Set it so anyone who gets the tag customer drops out of this sequence immediately. Otherwise someone who paid you on Tuesday still gets "are you still interested" on Friday.
  3. Then make a second, tiny workflow: when a payment succeeds, add tag customer. Two steps. That is the whole thing, and it saves you a very awkward message one day.

Will my emails land in spam?

Some will at first. Everyone's do. It is not something you did wrong.

Three things that help a lot. Send from a real business email, not a brand new free one. Do not put a link in the very first email. And do not write like an advertisement, which is exactly why we made them sound like you.

Send email 1 to yourself first and check where it lands before you switch the workflow on.

Can it send SMS instead? Mas binabasa ang text.

Yes, but not today and not for free. Sending SMS costs money per message and needs a separate setup.

Get the emails working first. They cost nothing. Add SMS later once you actually have people coming in.

Does Claude see my client information? Is my data private?

Be sensible. Whatever you paste into the terminal gets sent to Anthropic's servers to be processed, the same as anything you type into ChatGPT.

So: never paste passwords, card numbers, OTPs, or a client's ID documents. Not into this, not into any AI tool.

Pasting your own captions, your own prices and your own draft messages is fine. If you handle someone else's customer data under an agreement, check that agreement before you paste it anywhere.
